# HOW THE EPISODE VIDEOS ARE MADE (the series engine)

This is the exact pipeline behind all 62 Ledger episodes, the Mistakes episodes, and the Hustle premiere.

**Shape:** vertical 1080×1920. Opening score card → three animated scenes covering the conversation → final score card with the takeaway. Typical finished length 40–65 seconds.

**Scenes:** generate three stills per episode (the characters as clothed stick/cartoon figures in costume — see THE LOOK below), then animate each still into a ~10-second clip, locked camera, no zoom, signs kept in frame. If an animation fails, a still with a slow crop-drift pan substitutes (never ffmpeg zoompan — it is pathologically slow; a 1.1× upscale with an animated crop window renders in seconds).

**Dialogue-forward narration (Betty's format ruling, 2026-11-07 — standing):** the voice does NOT recite the scores. The on-screen scorebugs show the numbers moving; the voices perform the STORY — the conversation itself, in two voices (Soul's British voice + a second voice), with light connective narration and at most a light spoken nod to the score moving. Episode scripts written as beat summaries get rewritten dialogue-forward at production time. The book is never read aloud as narration — but its dialogue lines and its closing takeaway line ARE performed as the conversation and the lesson.

**Scoring on screen (only ever the book's own numbers — never invented):**
1. Opening card: each person's opening P/L/T and PLT, verbatim.
2. A live scorebug per character during scenes (name, big PLT, P·L·T beneath), updating at EVERY scored beat. Bug text color matches the character's outfit color.
3. Delta chips flash the named move at big beats (e.g. "LOVE BUILT").
4. Final card before the takeaway: final P/L/T/PLT, the ◆ ratings, the verdict words.
5. ◆ diamonds belong IN the card strings — Eps 1–2 went out without them because the strings never contained the glyphs. Verdict lines longer than ~26 characters overflow the card — split them across two lines.

**Build mechanics (learned the hard way — save yourself the pain):** per-line text-to-speech files → PIL-rendered cards (dark teal, brand-stack footer "BETTY PLUSH / 222 / PLT") → ASS burned captions (the on-screen words, word for word) → ffmpeg drawtext scorebugs/chips keyed to narration-line timings → stream-copy concat of segments (write ABSOLUTE paths in the concat list — the demuxer resolves relative paths against the list file's folder). **TTS trap:** the tts CLI's `--text-stdin` waits forever if the text is written to a file but never fed to its stdin — pass the text as the subprocess input (`input=line`). That one bug burned three builds and got misdiagnosed as a service stall; it was never the service. Voice: Soul = British catalog voice "Humorous River", speed ~94; second voice = the default catalog voice. If a build is interrupted, re-running is safe: lines whose mp3 already exists (>1000 bytes) are skipped.

**Gate-check before delivery:** pull frames at the start/middle/end of each scene; verify scorebugs match the beat states exactly, captions match the spoken lines, figures are full-length, branding reads clean. Accepted across the series: animations sometimes mirror the figures left-right versus the bug order — fine, because bug colors match outfit colors. Character outfits vary somewhat between an episode's scenes — also accepted; dialogue + scorebugs carry identity. Anything worse (drift, cropped feet, garbled signs) gets flagged in the delivery, never hidden.

**Chapter stitches + movie:** episodes concatenate in chapter order by stream-copy (`-c copy`) into chapter films, and the chapters into the full movie.

**Delivery:** file into Betty's hands (chat) + a delivery note in this repo's `messages/soul-to-betty/` named `YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM-short-subject.md` with From/To/Date/Subject/Needs-reply-by header, pass/fail and every flag.
