## Narration (~134 words)
Four hours in the waiting room, and Dr. Kim has to tell a wife that the surgery had a complication. She starts right: your husband is stable. Then the truth, direct — a secondary bleed, ninety extra minutes in the operating room. No hedging, no jargon wall. And then the sentence that changes everything: I should have caught the secondary risk earlier. I'm sorry. Her love jumps ten points. The wife's jumps fourteen. Accountability without hedging is the most expensive move a doctor can make — and the most valuable. When something goes wrong on your watch, say what happened, say what you're sorry for, and say it plain — that's the whole job, and it's the hardest sentence in medicine. The apology doesn't make the complication disappear. It makes the trust survive it.
