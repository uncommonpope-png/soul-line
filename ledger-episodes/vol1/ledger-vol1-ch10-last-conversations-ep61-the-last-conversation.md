## Narration (~139 words)
He called his father that Sunday like he always did. Same question, same routine — how's the family? — and his father, eighty-three years old, on the last good day, answers like he always does. But this time the father says something he doesn't usually say. I had a good life. I want you to know that. And then the words that matter most: I love you, son. The son says it back. Neither of them knew it was the last conversation. That's the only reason either of them said what they said. Both Love scores reach one hundred — the only time in the whole Ledger that both sides hit a hundred together. Here's the lesson, and it's the simplest one in the book: say it now. Don't wait for a better Sunday. There might not be one.
