## Narration (~134 words)
Sophia wants a mentor, and she's asking a person who has mentored exactly two people in twelve years — both of them vice presidents now. She doesn't open with her need. She opens with his constraint: I won't take much of your time. Then she names her goal plainly — run a profit-and-loss within five years — and says the sentence that wins the whole conversation: I know what I don't know. I need someone who does. He agrees to once a month, one question, his honest read. That's the deal, and the burden is on her to prepare. If you want a mentor, don't ask for their time — ask for their judgment, show them a real goal, and admit what you don't know. That's what makes a busy person invest in you.
