## Narration (~138 words)
The teacher called a meeting, and the parent walked in expecting a normal conference about grades. It isn't. Ms. Davis has been watching this child for two months, and what she's seeing isn't academic — he's withdrawn, he's lost weight, he doesn't want to come to school. Here's what makes this conversation work: she starts with what's true and good — he's one of the most perceptive kids she's taught — and then she makes the parent a partner, not a recipient of bad news. I wanted you to know what I'm seeing so you can see it at home too. And the parent says the line that saves the whole meeting: I've noticed things too. Here's the lesson: the best parent-teacher conversations are the ones where both people are saying the same thing at the same time.
