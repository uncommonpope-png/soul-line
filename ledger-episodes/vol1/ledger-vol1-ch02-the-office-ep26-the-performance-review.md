## Narration (~142 words)
Kevin is ninety days into a performance plan, and today is the conversation that decides whether he stays. Most people in that chair walk in defensive. Kevin walks in and opens with the last thing anyone expects: you were right about the deadline misses. Accountability, first sentence. Then he names what was going on at home, owns it, and says the operative words — it's handled now. Then the move that flips the whole meeting: he's already started the work, without being asked. His score climbs from forty-four to seventy point eight in one conversation. The meeting that starts with "you were right" almost always ends well. Own it, fix it, and show the work before they ask for it. That's how a review that could end a job becomes the meeting that saves it — accountability first, evidence second, excuses never.
