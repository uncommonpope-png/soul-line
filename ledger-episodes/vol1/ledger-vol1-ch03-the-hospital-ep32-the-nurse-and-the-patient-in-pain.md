## Narration (~136 words)
He says his pain is a ten. The chart says something different. The nurse has seen drug-seeking before — and she's also seen real pain dismissed. The patient opens with the sentence that tells you everything: "I know you see that a lot." He's been doubted before, and he's braced for it again. The nurse doesn't default to protocol. She asks for specifics — where, what it feels like, how long — and he gives her everything: left side, sharp when he breathes in, two days, getting worse. So she believes him, and calls the doctor. His tax drops eight points the moment the weight of being doubted lifts. When someone says their pain is a ten, ask what it feels like before you decide what it is. The nurse heard what was underneath the sentence.
