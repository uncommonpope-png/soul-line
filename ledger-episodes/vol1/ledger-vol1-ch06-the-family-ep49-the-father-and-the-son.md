## Narration (~145 words)
He came home different, and his father can see it. Sixty-one years old, a man who has watched his son struggle before, and he wants to ask what's wrong — but he knows pushing will shut the door. So he says something else instead. You don't seem right. I'm not asking to pry. I'm asking because I'm your father. The son deflects — I'm okay, Dad — and his father doesn't chase him. He just leaves the door open: you don't have to tell me, but if there's ever a day you want to, I'm not going anywhere. And that is the move. The son lets one true thing through — he lost something last year he hasn't learned how to talk about yet. Here's the lesson: when someone isn't ready, don't ask what the thing is. Just make it possible to say it someday.
