## Narration (~135 words)

He asks her to say more — tell me what you're thinking about — and then the question that moves the whole conversation from medical to human: what does home look like for you right now? Her answer is the highest love in the hospital chapter: my garden. My daughter's cooking. My cat on the bed. Ninety-four and ninety-six. She isn't choosing to die. She's choosing the life worth having. Those answers should always be available before any medical decision is made — ask what home looks like first. Let the person, not the chart, lead the conversation — because the life worth having is the one she just described.
