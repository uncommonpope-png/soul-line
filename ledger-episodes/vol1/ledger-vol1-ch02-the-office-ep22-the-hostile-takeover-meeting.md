## Narration (~148 words)
New owners bought the company, and the COO walks into his first meeting with them feeling twenty years of work hanging by a thread. The new majority owner doesn't open with cuts or demands. He opens with a question: tell me what I need to know that isn't in a document. The COO answers with three things — vendor relationships that need him for a year, two undocumented dependencies in the old tech, a morale problem in Chicago that will cost real money. Specific, with timelines and stakes. By the end of the meeting he doesn't just survive the takeover — he owns the Chicago problem and walks out indispensable. In any power transition, your institutional knowledge is your leverage. Show it in the first conversation, specific and unhedged. The new owners won't see a cost to cut — they'll see a person they can't afford to lose.
