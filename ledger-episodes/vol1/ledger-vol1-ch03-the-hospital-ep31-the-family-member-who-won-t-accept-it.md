## Narration (~144 words)
The prognosis is clear. The family isn't. Dr. Patel has given this family the same information three times, and the daughter keeps asking for a second opinion — because what she's really saying is, I hope you're wrong. The doctor doesn't argue. She supports the request, then asks the question underneath it: what do you hope a second opinion will tell you? And then she steps out of the doctor role for one sentence: I would want the same thing if it were my parent. The daughter's love jumps twelve points in that moment. Grief needs to be named before it can move. When someone can't accept the news, don't repeat the facts louder — name the grief, support the ask, and stay in the room either way. The daughter didn't need louder facts. She needed someone to stand in the grief with her.
