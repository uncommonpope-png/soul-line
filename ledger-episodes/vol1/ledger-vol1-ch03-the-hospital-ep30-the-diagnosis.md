## Narration (~139 words)
Mr. Hayes came in for a routine follow-up. The doctor starts with the line that tells him everything before a word about his health: I want to make sure we have enough time. Then the news — a mass in his left lung, malignant, stage two. His score craters: profit down fourteen, love down ten, tax up twenty in one sentence. That's what a diagnosis looks like on a scorecard. But watch the doctor: he doesn't rush, doesn't hide behind jargon. "This isn't a terminal diagnosis. We're going to fight this." The word "we" does as much work as the medicine. Sixty-eight percent five-year survival — more people who beat this than don't. His love score climbs from seventy to eighty during the worst conversation of his life. How you say it matters as much as what you say.
