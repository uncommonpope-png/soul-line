## Narration (~134 words)
Two hours to surgery, first time under the knife, and the patient asks the question behind every pre-surgery conversation: is this going to be okay? The surgeon doesn't offer empty comfort. He offers specifics: I've done this surgery over four hundred times. I know exactly what I'm doing in there. Then the patient asks the real question — what if something goes wrong? — and the surgeon answers with honesty and a number: we've trained for every scenario, and the chance of a significant complication in your case is under two percent. The patient's answer is the highest thing a patient can give a surgeon: I trust you. Comfort without data is noise. Specificity with experience is medicine. The patient who hears both can finally say the words that matter most: I trust you.
