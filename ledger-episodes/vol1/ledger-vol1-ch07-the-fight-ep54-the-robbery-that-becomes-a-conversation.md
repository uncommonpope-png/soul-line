## Narration (~140 words)
Closing time at the bodega. A kid walks in, terrified, first time, with a plan that isn't a plan — and Ray has been behind that counter for twenty years. Ray's Profit isn't the money in the register. His Profit is getting home. So he does the last thing the kid expects. He names him a person — young man — and he hands over every dollar, no fight, no hero move. Then he says the one word that stops the whole robbery: son. You don't need to do this. Go home, young man. And the kid does. He walks out with the money still in Ray's hands and a different choice in his head. Here's the lesson: Ray's Profit climbed from forty-four to sixty-eight by giving money away. Sometimes the highest move in the room is refusing the fight.
