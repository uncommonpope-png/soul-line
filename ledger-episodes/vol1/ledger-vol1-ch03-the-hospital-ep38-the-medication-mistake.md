## Narration (~145 words)
A father is sitting by his daughter's bed when the charge nurse comes to tell him the wrong dose of pain medication was given. She doesn't wait for him to find out from someone else. She says it directly — what happened, that it was caught quickly, that his daughter is stable — and then the apology. He asks the question every parent asks: how does that happen? She answers honestly: a documentation error on the handoff, already reported, changes being documented. And then the line that holds the trust together: that's not enough, but it's what happens immediately. Acknowledging the inadequacy before the family has to name it — that's the move that preserves trust when trust has every reason to break. Say it first, say it straight, and say what happens next — honesty is the only version of this conversation that works.
