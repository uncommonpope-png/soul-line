## Narration (~136 words)
She's getting married, and her mother has been holding a worry for six months. Not a small one — a real one, about the man, about how he goes quiet when things get hard. So the mother asks for the floor before her daughter can answer: hear me out first. And she says it, plainly, honestly. Then her daughter answers with the sentence that wins the whole conversation. I know his flaws. I choose them. She's not blind. She's not asking permission. She's asking to be seen — and her mother hears it. The mother's Love jumps ten points in that one exchange. Here's the lesson: a grown child's choice isn't a request for approval. It's a request to be witnessed. When you hear the choice instead of fighting it, the love climbs on both sides.
