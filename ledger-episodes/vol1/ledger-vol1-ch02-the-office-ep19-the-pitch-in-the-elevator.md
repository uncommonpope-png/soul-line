## Narration (~137 words)
Three years of work, five minutes in an elevator, one shot at the CFO. Most people would open with "I have an idea." Marcus opens with his name, his team, and a line that makes the CFO lean in: he's built something the man should know about. Then the numbers do the talking — a model that caught fourteen accounts about to leave, three of them top twenty. No adjectives, no hype, just results, recent and counted. The CFO doesn't ask for a pitch. He asks for the deck, today. That's the lesson: abstract claims open doors, specific results get meetings. Before you step into any elevator, know your numbers cold — how many, how much, how recent — because five floors is all you get, and a real result beats a great idea every single time.
