## Narration (~135 words)
Third overdose this year, and the patient wakes up braced for the lecture he knows is coming: I know what you're going to say. Dr. James doesn't say it. He asks a question instead: what happened tonight? The patient answers with the simplest, truest sentence in the chapter: I had it handled. And then I didn't. Then the doctor asks two words — not "you need help," not "you should get help." Do you want help? The patient says yeah. I do. The conversation that saves a life often looks like two lines. Ask, don't tell. The answer to a question belongs to the person being asked — and that's why it worked. The patient chose help instead of having it forced on him, and a choice, once made, is a door that stays open.
