## Narration (~140 words)
Ten years old, hands in his pockets, counting a dollar sixty-three for a two-dollar pack of candy — that's DeShawn at the corner store after school. His auntie Monique watches him count it twice. Then she sees it: the other pocket. Something small and square pushing against the fabric. "DeShawn. What's in your other pocket?" The whole store freezes. A pack of gum. Two dollars at most, sitting on the counter like a television. Monique doesn't yell — low and calm is scarier than angry, and DeShawn knows it. She pays for the candy and the gum, and she gives him work: back Saturday to sweep Leroy's floor. Outside she tells him the real rule. "Shame don't teach nothing. Work teaches. Sorry teaches. You don't steal from yourself." The debt got paid, and his name stayed clean on the corner.
