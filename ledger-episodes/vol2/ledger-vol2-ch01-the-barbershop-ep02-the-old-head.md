## Narration (~149 words)
The shop is empty except for you and the old head, waiting in the chairs — not for a cut, for a talk. You tell him you charge twenty-five, trying to keep it fair. He asks: fair to who? You've got rent. You've got a baby coming. Fair is what keeps the lights on. You say if you raise it, they'll go to Dre's chair. He says some will — good. He watched you fix a boy's hairline last week, the one Dre wouldn't touch, a man about to walk into a job interview looking crazy, and you saved him. That's not a twenty-five-dollar cut. That's forty, minimum. Keep your price low and people think your work is low — every cheap cut tells the room you're cheap. Your pops worked two jobs till his knees gave out. Don't repeat that part. Monday, you say. Thirty-five. Same as Dre.
