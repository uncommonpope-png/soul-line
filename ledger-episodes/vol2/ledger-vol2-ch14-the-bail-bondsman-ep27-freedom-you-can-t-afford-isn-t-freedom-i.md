## Narration (~149 words)
By the time Marcus walks out of the bail bondsman's office, he's already lost: eight hundred dollars gone, his mother's car title signed over, and a court date he can't miss. But he didn't lose today. He lost three months ago, the night he ran with the wrong crew and told himself it was just one night. Frank Deluca has been in this business forty years. He doesn't raise his voice. He tells Marcus the truth: your mama put her car up, son. If you don't show up in court, your mama walks to work. That's what it means. Everybody swears in this chair, Frank says. The chair don't care about swearing. The chair cares about showing up. Marcus leaves smaller than he came in — and that's not an insult. That's the beginning of learning. Freedom you can't afford isn't freedom. It's a loan your family pays back.
