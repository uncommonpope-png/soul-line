## Narration (149 words)
A widow is standing at the graveside, and a friend has been waiting by his car for twenty minutes, working up the nerve. "Mrs. Johnson? My name's Earl. I knew your husband forty years. And I owe him money. A thousand dollars, from nineteen-eighty-nine. I never paid it back, and I never forgot it." She stares at him. "You came to a funeral to tell me that?" "No, ma'am. I came to a funeral to PAY it." He holds out an envelope — a thousand dollars, plus interest, because forty years is a long time to carry a debt. Her hands shake when she takes it. Not because of the money. Because in forty years, nobody came back to make it right — and this old man just did, on the worst day of her life. Pay it late, but pay it. A debt doesn't die with the man.
