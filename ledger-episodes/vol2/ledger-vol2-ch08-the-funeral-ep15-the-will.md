## Narration (144 words)
The funeral hasn't even ended — potato salad on paper plates in the church basement — and already the brother and sister are in the hallway, voices low and sharp. "She left the house to ME." "I paid the taxes on it for five years." Their mother has been dead six days, and her children are arguing over the house she lived in for forty years. Then Denise stops. Breathes. "Mama wouldn't want this. Not here. Not today." And James looks at the floor and says the real sentence: "I don't want to lose the house AND her in the same month." That's not money talking. That's grief, wearing a money costume. Denise hears it. "Then we don't sell it. We keep it. Both our names." They walk back into the repast together. Hear the grief under the argument. The house stays the house.
