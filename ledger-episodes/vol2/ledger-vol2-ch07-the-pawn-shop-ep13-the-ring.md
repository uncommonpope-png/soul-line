## Narration (141 words)
A man slides a small velvet box across the pawn-shop counter. Inside: his grandmother's gold ring, worn thin on the band. He needs rent money. The pawnbroker offers two hundred, and the man's voice cracks — it's real gold, it's got to be worth more. And the broker says it gentle: it's worth more to you than to me. To me, it's weight and a stone. The man asks for three hundred. Please. Just three. The broker counts out three hundreds — and gives him ninety days to come back for it. Ninety days. He holds the ring; he doesn't sell it. That extra hundred bought something no scale can weigh. The broker walked away with a ring he may never sell, and the man walked out with rent money and a deadline. The best pawnbrokers sell time, not just things.
