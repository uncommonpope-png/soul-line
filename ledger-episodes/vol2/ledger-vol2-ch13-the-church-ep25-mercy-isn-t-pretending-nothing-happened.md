## Narration (~148 words)
What do you say to a man who stole from the collection plate — and then came back to sit in the same pew? Walter is sixty-two. Usher for eleven years. Two hundred and forty dollars missing. Pastor Greene asks him to walk after service. Walter doesn't cry. He goes quiet in the way men go quiet when the excuse they rehearsed all week falls apart in their mouth. His lights were getting cut off. His grandbaby's medicine. He told himself he'd put it back. The pastor believes him — and names it plain: the plate was short three Sundays, not one. Walter says it was three. And that's the turn. Pay it back, a little at a time, and step down from ushering while you do. Not as punishment. As protection. Mercy isn't pretending nothing happened. It's naming it plain — and still leaving the door open.
