## Narration (~146 words)
She already knows how this ends, and that's the whole problem. You're the prosecutor in the hallway, and you lay it out flat, like a weather report: eighteen months, take it today, or trial Monday, four years. Her lawyer doesn't flinch. His client found a discrepancy and reported it — your own audit says the account was already short before she touched it. Then he leans in low, and the whole hallway leans with him. A twenty-six-year-old woman flags a theft, and your office charges her because the real thief had a better lawyer — you really want to tell that to twelve people from this neighborhood? You pause half a second. That's the tell. Twelve months, probation, no jail. He counters: six months, charge drops. Nine months, you say. Final. She nods fast. Done. A deal in a hallway beats a gamble in a courtroom.
