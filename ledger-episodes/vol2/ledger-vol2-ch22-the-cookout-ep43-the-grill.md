## Narration (~142 words)
Two hours late, empty-handed, sunglasses on — that's how Sam rolls into his sister Denise's cookout, then reaches straight for a plate. Denise has been up since seven, bought the meat, worked the grill, fed forty people. She puts the spatula down and turns. "Sam, you thirty-eight years old. You got a job, a car, two hands — and every cookout you eat first." The whole backyard goes quiet. Then she draws the line, calm as Sunday: "I'm going to feed you. After the elders eat. After the kids eat. After the people who brought something eat." Sam could've left. Instead he sits by the cooler, starts carrying chairs, and waits his turn. An hour later Denise walks him a plate herself. "Next time, bring the ice. Ten dollars. That's the ticket in." He stayed, he waited, and the cookout stayed hers.
