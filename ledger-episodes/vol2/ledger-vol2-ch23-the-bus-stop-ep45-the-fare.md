## Narration (~147 words)
Seven in the morning, bus stop on Poplar and Cleveland, and nineteen-year-old Julian taps his card at the front of the line — red light. He's seventy-five cents short of the two-dollar fare, his shift starts in twenty minutes, and the driver's got a schedule. Miss Gloria, sixty-eight, church hat on a Tuesday, stands and walks to the front. She digs past the mints and the little Bible and presses a crisp dollar into his hand. "You don't have to know me to take a dollar. Just pay it forward when somebody else is short." He rides, and she gives him the rule with the money: two dollars in your sock every morning before you leave the house. "Your bus money is not spending money." Right there on the bus, Julian folds his last dollar into his sock. A dollar spent right can save a whole job.
