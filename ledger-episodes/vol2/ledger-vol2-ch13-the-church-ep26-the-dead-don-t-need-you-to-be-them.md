## Narration (~149 words)
The choir's gone home. The organ's covered. And Keisha is sitting alone in the front pew with her grandmother's funeral program in her lap — three days ago she buried her. A woman named Mother Ruth, eighty-four and sharp as a razor, sits down next to her without asking. Keisha doesn't know how to be in this family without her grandmother. Mother Ruth doesn't give her a plan. She gives her something better: permission. Grief doesn't come with instructions, she says. It just moves in and rearranges the furniture. You don't have to know today. You just have to show up. And when Keisha says she can't sing like her grandmother, Mother Ruth says: nobody's asking you to sing like her. They're asking you to sing like you. The dead don't need you to be them. They need you to take your seat — and sing your own note.
