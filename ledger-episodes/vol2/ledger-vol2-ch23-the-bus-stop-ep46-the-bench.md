## Narration (~149 words)
Five-thirty in the evening, same bus stop, different world — sun low and mean, bench hot enough to cook on. Alisha's been waiting a long time: baby on her hip, grocery bag tipping over, phone dead. The next bus is twenty minutes out, maybe more. Wallace walks up — work boots, hard hat in hand — and does something simple. He takes off his dusty canvas jacket, folds it, and sets it on the bench. "Sit the baby on that. It's softer than the bench, and it ain't burning hot." Then he holds her grocery bag like it's his own and stands there blocking the sun, big as a tree. Twenty minutes of not waiting alone. When the bus finally comes, he hands her up the steps, baby asleep on his jacket. Being seen at a bus stop costs nothing — and sometimes it's the whole day for somebody.
