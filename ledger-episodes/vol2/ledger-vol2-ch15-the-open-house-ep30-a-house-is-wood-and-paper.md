## Narration (~145 words)
The last visitor of the day is an old man named Henry, seventy-nine, who doesn't sign the sheet. He just walks room to room, touching the doorframes like he's reading braille. He grew up in this house. His mother planted the rose bush out back — it's still there. She used to hum in the kitchen while the bread was rising. Carla doesn't pitch. She doesn't hover. She doesn't mention square footage. She just steps back and lets him have the room. When he asks if they're tearing it down, she tells him the truth: the offer came in cash. Then the rose bush goes too, he says. Not if I can help it, she says. Give me your number. I'll ask. A house is wood and paper — but the stories inside it are the real square footage, and somebody has to protect them.
