### The Premise

*The Ledger* proved every conversation has a score. *The Hustle* proved every deal does. This book proves every MOVE does.

Forty-eight laws, written on the block and priced in the open. Laws of the stoop, the table, the deal, the chat, the shop, the room, love, the Tax, the stage, the elders, war — and the last four, which price all the others. Each law gets one sketch: somebody lives it, somebody breaks it, and the score settles what the move was actually worth.

Uncle Ledger and Ms. Verdict host. They state each law cold, from the pink book, before the sketch begins — and they rule when it ends. Around them rotates the whole family of these books: Trey, Marlo, June, Mama Lou, Uncle Ray, Big Frame, Auntie Gladys, Dee, and — once, in the last chapter, under the last law — The Collector herself.

The comedy is the surface, and it's meant to be. The philosophy is the engine: every move a person makes is priced in Profit, Love and Tax. Power is funny until the Tax comes due.

**A note on the laws themselves:** all forty-eight are original to this book — written from the stoop, the shop, the table and the chat, in this book's own words, and priced in PLT. They borrow no other book's text, titles or list. If a law sounds like something your grandmother said, that's because she was right first.

### How the Score Works

- **P — Profit.** What a person is gaining: money, ground, attention, advantage.
- **L — Love.** Trust, loyalty, reputation — the infrastructure every future move stands on. It compounds over years and burns in minutes.
- **T — Tax.** What the move is costing: time, stress, obligation, goodwill, dignity.
- **PLT** moves as the three move. It rises when Profit and Love rise and Tax falls; it falls the other way. The number is the move, told honestly.
- **◆ ratings** run one to five, by final PLT: ◆ under 35 · ◆◆ 35–54.9 · ◆◆◆ 55–69.9 · ◆◆◆◆ 70–84.9 · ◆◆◆◆◆ 85 and above.

### The Pink Book — Locked In

In every episode, the pink book is in frame — the laws live in it, and it travels the way it does across the whole series: opened for the law card, open on the counter during the sketch, closed on the ruling. It sits beside the Betty Plush bunny bookbag, same design, set as the series prop, never renamed, never drifting. Where a law is read, the pink book is already there. Products that appear are the real souls, tagged as themselves — Spike, Drippy, Mendie, Denim Patch, Ember Stripe, Pixel Heart, Bell Paws, Nightwing Dawn, Listen — never stand-ins.

### A Note on the Scripts

Each law is followed by its episode script: who's in the room, the beats, the on-screen words, the score after, and the takeaway — the book's closing line, verbatim. Production follows the house rules: the dialogue is performed, the scores live on screen, the narration tells the story, and nothing publishes without Ms GCP's approval of the exact post. The **Explainer Line** in each script is a DRAFT sitting in Pope's slot — a candidate written to be replaced by Pope's own word, never presented as it.

All characters are fictional. No real brands, no real people. The moves are universal; the arithmetic is the book's.
