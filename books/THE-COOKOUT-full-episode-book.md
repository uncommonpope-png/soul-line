### The Premise

*The Ledger* proved every conversation has a score. *The Hustle* proved every deal is a conversation. *The Cookout* proves every family is an economy — and the yard is where its books are kept.

Somebody brings the meat. Somebody brings a store-bought pie in a homemade dish. Somebody shows up with a plate already in his hand. Somebody was there at seven in the morning, setting out chairs nobody will ever thank her for. A cookout looks like a party. It is a market, a bank, and a court, all running at the same time, all scored in the same three numbers.

This book scores the whole day: the invite list, the sign-up sheet, the law of the grill, the plate economy, the ice tax, the ones who work, the ones who don't, grace, the leftover treaty — and the real ledger Mama Lou reads after the yard empties. The philosophy is the same one it always is: Love is not a feeling at the cookout. Love is infrastructure. It is who carries the gathering, and it compounds in plates, in rides, in chairs stacked after dark.

Laugh at the violations. They are universal. Then check your own name on the sign-up sheet.

### How the Score Works

- **P — Profit.** What a person is gaining: food, standing, comfort, ground at the table.
- **L — Love.** Trust, loyalty, reputation — the infrastructure every future gathering stands on. It compounds over years of showing up and burns in one afternoon of empty hands.
- **T — Tax.** What the day is costing: labor, money, heat, patience, the carrying nobody sees.
- **PLT** moves as the three move. It rises when Profit and Love rise and Tax falls; it falls the other way. The number is the conversation, told honestly.
- **◆ ratings** run one to five, by final PLT: ◆ under 35 · ◆◆ 35–54.9 · ◆◆◆ 55–69.9 · ◆◆◆◆ 70–84.9 · ◆◆◆◆◆ 85 and above.

### The Pink Book — Locked In

In every episode, the pink book — the Betty Plush bunny bookbag — is in frame, same design, set as the series prop, the way it is locked in across *The Ledger* and *The Hustle*. It does not drift, it is never renamed, and it appears in every room this book enters: by the grill, on the sign-up table, at the elders' table, in the kitchen at the dish pit. Where the family gathers, the pink book is already there.

### A Note on the Scripts

This book was written as a script from the start — the book and the episode script are one document, not an adaptation. Each conversation is a performable scene: dialogue-first, five scored beats, each beat one clip. Following each conversation is its episode script: who's in the room, the beats, the on-screen words (lines a character actually says, written to be carried as captions verbatim), the score after, and the takeaway — the book's closing line, verbatim. Production follows the house rules: the dialogue is performed, the scores live on screen, the narration tells the story, and nothing publishes without Ms GCP's approval of the exact post. The **Explainer Line** in each script is a DRAFT sitting in Pope's slot — a candidate written to be replaced by Pope's own word, never presented as it.

All characters are fictional. No real brands, no real people. The cookout is every cookout — the conversation is the subject.
